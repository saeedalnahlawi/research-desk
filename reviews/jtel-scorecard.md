The point of keeping this file is not to claim a hit. It is to record, in public, what the model got right
and what it got wrong, and to keep doing it for every note. A valuation nobody ever revisits is an opinion
with a spreadsheet attached.

## What this means, honestly

1. **The check was right.** The audited capex was 84.7m, not the 72.1m forecast line. Recomputing from there
   was correct.
2. **The conclusion was too conservative.** The market has run to 3.89 and peaked at 4.45, which is where the
   valuation would sit with capex at roughly 20% rather than 23.5%.
3. **The error was not arithmetic, it was judgement.** Correcting the base year without asking whether that
   year was representative moved the answer by a third. One heavy investment year is not a normal year, and a
   five-year forecast should not be built on it without saying so.
4. **What would have been better:** publish the valuation as a range from the start (which is now the standard
   in `docs/how-i-work.md`), with capex as an explicit variable rather than a point, and the 2023-is-a-peak
   question stated in the note instead of being asked after publication.

## What would change the scorecard next

- A re-forecast with capex normalised to ~20% and the same WACC: fair value moves to roughly 3.9, which is
  where the market is. If that version is published and the price then falls back below 3, the normalisation
  was wrong and the original 23.5% was right.
- Watch FY2025 capex as a share of revenue when the audited statements arrive. Two heavy years in a row would
  settle the argument in favour of the original assumption.

Keep this file updated at each set of annual results. It is the only part of the work that cannot be faked later.
